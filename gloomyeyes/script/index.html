INTRO

The Pheasant and the Gravekeeper

Shadow Miniature Prologue · Cemetery ~3 MIN
v009 — NEW INTRO

Darkness. Low fog between the graves.

Something moves among the headstones. Out of the fog steps a bird — a Swinhoe's pheasant. Dark plumage with a bluish sheen, a red crest, a few white markings. It circles us, stops, tilts its head — one eye, then the other. A short, dry squawk. Another. As if it's sizing us up and isn't impressed.

It walks to a headstone further back. Behind the stone is a freshly dug hole with a shovel stuck in the dirt. The pheasant stretches its neck over the stone and calls, long and insistent, down into the pit. Like it's calling someone.

Silence. Another call. Louder.

GRAVEKEEPER (FROM THE PIT)

Coming… I'm coming.

A shovelful of dirt flies up over the headstone. Another. A hand appears, then a lantern, then at last the Gravekeeper, hauling himself out of the grave. He brushes the dirt off his coat.

GRAVEKEEPER

What is it? Did somebody die? Because if nobody died, I'm busy.

The pheasant squawks. He ignores it, sets the lantern on the stone.

GRAVEKEEPER

If you're here for the worms, get in line. The dead got here first.

The pheasant squawks louder and stretches toward us. He sighs, turns around annoyed… and sees us. He goes still. Lifts the lantern. Studies us a long moment. The moths begin to circle the flame.

GRAVEKEEPER (to the pheasant, quietly)

These? You really think it's these ones?

The pheasant pecks his feet. Once, twice. Hops in place, flapping.

GRAVEKEEPER

Ow! All right, all right…

He rubs his ankle, looks us over head to toe, unimpressed.

GRAVEKEEPER

I've buried people who looked better.
(sighs) If you say they're special… they're special.

He sits on the headstone, lantern at his feet. Takes a breath, like someone starting a story he's told too many times.

GRAVEKEEPER

A story needs light to be told. I'll tell this one with what's left of it.

The sun is gone.

A pause. It stretches. Too long. He stares at the shovel.

GRAVEKEEPER

…that handle's loose.

The pheasant pecks his boot.

GRAVEKEEPER

Ah! Right, right. The sun. The sun matters, because without it there's nothing. Nothing at all.

He reaches into a bag of unearthed things by the pit. Pulls out an old fork, bent and rusted, and holds it to the lantern. Its shadow falls huge across the headstone.

GRAVEKEEPER

Humans did this. Took too much from the world, and the world stopped giving.
(tosses the fork over his shoulder) And then they complained about the service.

He digs in the bag again. Plants a twisted, mossy root in the dirt before the lantern. Its shadow straightens on the stone, clumsy, like someone standing for the first time.

GRAVEKEEPER (pointing at us)

You're what came after.
Zombies. Nature's answer to what humans broke. Not the elegant one.

He looks up from the root, out at the dark.

GRAVEKEEPER

People are afraid of the dead that walk. Strange.
They should be more afraid of the living who decide who gets to walk.

The pheasant walks to the lantern, grips the handle in its beak and lifts it high over the graves. Light washes the cemetery. The moths go wild. The light grows beyond what a lantern could, and when it comes down, everything is miniature.

A small sun crosses a dimming sky. Below, the world comes apart: chunks of earth break loose and fall, things unfasten like pieces that no longer fit. The sun sinks, slowly, toward mountains cloaked in forest and mist, and hides among them.

GRAVEKEEPER (V.O.)

But the sun didn't run just anywhere. It took shelter in the mountains of Alishan — their forests, their mist. A sacred place. It chose to be the last thing left standing, while the rest of the world began to crumble.

Among the trees, a tall man in a long coat appears. Searching. Digging. He climbs, strikes the rock, sinks his hands into the earth. Every time he nears the light, the pheasant hides it a little more, and the man is left closing his hands around nothing.

GRAVEKEEPER (V.O.)

There's a man hunting the sun. Not to bring it back — to keep its power for himself. They call him the Uncle. He's no one's uncle.

From behind a rock, a small boy leans out, hunched, with two points of light where his eyes should be. The Uncle spins around. When he sees those eyes, he takes a step back.

GRAVEKEEPER (V.O.)

And there's a boy. One of yours. Gloomy. He carries something in his eyes that frightens the Uncle.

Gloomy looks straight at us. Then he scatters into a handful of fireflies that drift up through the fog. The miniature goes dark with them.

We're back in the cemetery. The pheasant lowers the lantern to the Gravekeeper's feet. He stands. Lifts it. Turns toward his pit, as if he's finished. The pheasant pecks his boot. One last time. He stops. Looks at us over his shoulder.

GRAVEKEEPER

If you get lost — and you will — look for the smallest light you can find. Big things in this world lie. Small ones don't have the time.

When in doubt… follow the fireflies.
(climbing down into the pit) And if there are no fireflies, you know where to find me.

The fireflies drift off among the graves. The pheasant lets out a squawk and looks at us, waiting.

Darkness.

v009: Replaces the 007 intro ("The Hiding of the Sun") with the pheasant-and-Gravekeeper framing from the new intro artifact, trimmed ~10%. The pheasant is the Gravekeeper's reluctant prompt; the story is built from unearthed objects and shadow-puppets. Curated Gravekeeper lines woven in: "A story needs light to be told…", "They should be more afraid of the living…", "…look for the smallest light you can find."

SCENE 01

The Dungeons — Execution Order

Underground Cages + Freight Elevator ~5 MIN

Cold iron. The smell of mildew and rust. We are in cages, side by side, in the dark. Through the bars — humans dressed as rabbits stand guard.

The Uncle enters carrying a cup of coffee. He takes a sip — gags — spits it out.

UNCLE

Who made this disgusting coffee?!

He turns on the bunny guards.

UNCLE

Was it you? Or you? Or YOU?
Did you put a rabbit in this? Be honest.

Behind him, Nina enters carrying another cup. She doesn't see the rage. Or pretends not to.

NINA

I made it!

The Uncle freezes. The rage doesn't fade — it gets covered with a smile, fast, the way you cover food before guests arrive.

UNCLE

Nina! I can't believe it. Such talent.
I taste fresh notes — like cypress wood. Light. Complex.
Silky. With a long, lingering aftertaste.

Nina holds up a small can. Reads the label flat.

NINA

I put shoe polish in it.

The Uncle holds back a visible gag. Swallows. Keeps smiling. Nina drifts past him toward our cages. She looks at us. Really looks. Not afraid.

Behind her back, the Uncle hands the cup to one of the bunny guards. His voice drops.

UNCLE

Drink it. All of it.

The bunny stares at the cup like it's a bullet. The Uncle doesn't move. Behind them, Nina has reached the bars of our cage. She turns back.

NINA

What's going to happen to them?

UNCLE

Nothing.

He doesn't look at her. He's still watching the bunny drink.

NINA

So you're going to release them?

The Uncle finally turns. The smile is gone.

UNCLE

Yes, Nina. I'll release them. From everything.
Starting with the weight of still existing.

Nina holds the bars. Doesn't speak. The Uncle keeps going.

UNCLE

Look at them. They're disgusting. I can't stand the sight of them.

NINA

They don't seem so ugly to me. I don't even think they're so different.

The Uncle's face tightens. He grabs the nearest bunny guard by the hood and yanks it off.

UNCLE

Of course they are. We are very different. Aesthetically superior.

He looks at the bunny's face. The bunny's face is the most hideous thing imaginable. The Uncle squeaks and shoves the hood back on as fast as he can.

UNCLE

They're dangerous. They're a plague. We wipe them all out. Every single one.

NINA

They don't look dangerous to me.

UNCLE

Enough. I'm sick of your questions. Do you want to end up like them? DEAD?

NINA

I didn't do anything wrong!

The Uncle's hand whips out. He punches the closest bunny in the face — a guard who had nothing to do with anything. The bunny crumples. The Uncle points at Nina. His finger is shaking.

UNCLE

Look what you make me do.

UNCLE

You. Get her out of here.

A bunny takes Nina by the arm and steers her toward the door. She plants her feet at the threshold and turns back toward the Uncle.

NINA

Leave them alone!

Her voice cracks on the last word. She means it. Then she's gone. The Uncle turns to us. Slowly. The friendly tyrant is gone. What's left is colder, quieter, and without mercy.

UNCLE

Kill them. Now.

He walks out without looking back.

v009 note: Same shoe-polish gag and cruelty as 007, with the curated "rabbit in this?" and "release them… from the weight of still existing" lines added. One change to serve the new arc: Nina isn't dragged off to a cell — she's escorted out. She's the Uncle's niece and (secretly) his spy, so she stays mobile in his world. Her sympathy here is real; her loyalty is the open question.

THE ESCAPE

Three bunny guards line up outside our cage. One has a heavy axe. One drags a rusted saw. The third grips a golf driver like a sword.

Behind them, deep in the corridor, something growls. Two glowing eyes appear in the dark. The three bunnies freeze. The axe bunny puffs his chest out, tries to look brave, walks into the dark.

He doesn't come out. A sound — short, wet — then nothing. The other two stare into the dark. Then at each other.

SAW BUNNY

…you go.

GOLF BUNNY

Why me?

SAW BUNNY

You have the bigger weapon.

GOLF BUNNY

It's a golf club.

SAW BUNNY

It's still bigger.

A long pause. Neither moves. Then, to the dark, Saw Bunny calls out, gently:

SAW BUNNY

We know you're there. And we deeply respect your personal space.

GOLF BUNNY

…maybe we go together?

SAW BUNNY

Together. Yes. Together.

They grab each other's hands and step into the dark. It swallows them at once. Two small sounds. Then nothing.

A wind moves through the dungeon. The torches blow out one by one. The dungeon goes black. Right beside our cage — an axe strikes iron. Once. Twice. The lock breaks. The door swings open.

We don't see him. But we feel him. Right next to us. Watching. Then he moves. Fast. He doesn't wait. We follow the sound of his footsteps deeper into the dark.

Carried from 007, tightened. Curated line added: Saw Bunny's "We know you're there. And we deeply respect your personal space."

We move through a stone tunnel, away from the dungeons, into a vast vertical shaft. A massive wooden freight elevator waits at our level. Battered. Ancient. Big enough for all of us. We walk in. It begins to rise. Slowly.

As we climb, we pass level after level of the Uncle's underground world.

LEVEL — THE MINE

A massive mining operation. Bunny humans hack at the walls and haul stone carts. They are looking for the sun.

LEVEL — THE NIGHT MARKET

The bunnies' living quarters and a communal mess hall, lifted from a Taiwanese night market. Steaming carts. Skewers. Dumplings. Noodles.

LEVEL — THE GUARDS

A guard post. A bunny on duty looks up, sees us rising, does a double-take, sounds the alarm.

Arrows begin flying at us. Some hit the wood. Some hit us. We feel nothing. Then — Gloomy moves. We don't see him. We see what he does. A bunny on a platform falls. Then another. He moves through their ranks like a shadow. We track him only by the bunnies that drop.

The elevator keeps rising. The shaft opens into stone. For the first time, we step outside.

Interactivity: Arrows fly toward us in the elevator — we can grab them mid-flight, pull them out of our zombie bodies, and throw them back. We feel no pain.

SCENE 02

First Light — Meeting Gloomy

Surface + River Crossing ~4 MIN

We are outside. The air up here is different — cold, damp, full of sounds we haven't heard in a long time. Leaves moving. Wind through trees. Something alive. For a long moment, no one moves. There is no sun. The sky is grey. But it goes on forever.

And then, behind us, we feel him. He's been with us the whole time. Now, for the first time, he steps into view.

Gloomy. Small. Hunched. His clothes stitched from things he found. His eyes glow faintly. His body tense, ready to run. The axe he used to free us still in his hand. He looks at us — his own kind — like he isn't sure what we are. Friends? Enemies? He hisses softly. We don't move. He turns and gestures for us to follow, keeping his distance. He won't let any of us close.

GRAVEKEEPER (V.O.)

He's been alone so long he forgot the difference between a friend and an enemy. Give him time. Trust is learned all over again, like walking.

Carried from 007. Curated Gravekeeper V.O. added over the reveal.

He leads us downhill, through rocky tunnels. The sound of water grows. The tunnels open onto a quiet river, where a wooden raft sits half-pulled out of the water. He prepared this. He was ready for us. We board. The current takes us, and we drift into the forest of Alishan.

Tall cypresses line the river. Grey light breaks through the canopy and trembles on the water. We can hear birds. Real birds. For the first time since the dungeons, the world feels alive.

Interactivity: Gloomy gestures for us to shift our weight side to side to steer the raft through the current.

SCENE 03

Gloomy's Home — The Mission

Refuge + Cemetery ~4 MIN

The group arrives at Gloomy's home, built by hand over time — platforms, planks, and ramps stacked in uneven layers. As we enter, a small wild boar charges Gloomy. For a moment it looks aggressive, then it's clearly play. The boar bumps him, circles, calms, and comes to sniff the group. Clearly his pet.

Inside, drawings cover every surface. Many show Nina. One lies on a table and Gloomy awkwardly tries to hide it, embarrassed. Others show a large glowing figure sending out beams of light — his dream of freeing the sun.

v009 — REWORKED MISSION

The group moves out to the nearby cemetery. The Gravekeeper is already there, kneeling beside an open hole. He's been digging. Around him, on the dirt, are things he's unearthed — a small bird skull, a faded photograph, a folded piece of paper. He doesn't put them back. He doesn't take them anywhere. He just keeps them in front of him, untouched.

Gloomy approaches cautiously. The Gravekeeper looks up.

GRAVEKEEPER

So. You made it.
Few people walk out of down there. Usually I'm the one who carries them up.

He looks past Gloomy, toward a thin line of smoke on the horizon — a railway.

GRAVEKEEPER

The sun didn't die. It hid — high in these mountains, deep in the rock. And there's only one thing in this whole valley that climbs that high.

He nods toward the smoke.

GRAVEKEEPER

The Uncle's train. He built it to haul what he digs up toward the peak. The engine is the only thing strong enough to reach the sun.

So that's what you do. You board it. You get to the front. And you take the locomotive.

He reaches for a bundle of old rabbit uniforms and hands them out.

GRAVEKEEPER

You can't move like this through his territory. These will let you walk among them.
A word of advice: walk slow. His rabbits walk like it hurts to exist. Walk with hope and they'll spot you in a second.

He keeps his eyes on the unearthed things a beat too long. He knows more about the sun than he's saying. But there's a train to catch.

v009: The mission changes. In 007 the locomotive was a weapon to destroy and Nina a prisoner to rescue. Now the sun is hidden high in the mountains and the Uncle's train is the only thing that climbs there — so the goal is to steal the locomotive. No "Nina is captured." The Gravekeeper's buried-objects mystery is kept. Curated lines woven: "So. You made it. Few people walk out of down there…" and "walk slow… walk with hope and they'll spot you."

A short, awkward training moment: the group tries to stand, move, and behave like humans. Gloomy shares a disguise with the boar, standing on top of it to seem taller, wobbling to keep his balance — ridiculous. When the group is ready, the Gravekeeper steps aside and plays a short, unnecessary melody on his harmonica. It serves no purpose. In the distance, the sound of the train begins to approach.

Interactivity: We must copy the Gravekeeper's choreography to pass as humans rather than zombies.

SCENE 04

The Train — The Heist

Alishan Forest Railway ~7 MIN
v009 — REWORKED SCENE

We don't take the platform. We come up on the train from behind, in the dark, and climb onto the rear car unseen. The locomotive is all the way at the front. To reach it, we have to cross every wagon on the train.

Inside, the space is narrow and tense. We follow Gloomy from car to car, bumping softly into walls and door frames the disguises were never built for. Soldiers move in lines around us. We keep our heads down and copy their tired, aching walk.

Two bunny soldiers murmur as one of their own sails past a window — the Uncle's handiwork, somewhere up the train.

BUNNY SOLDIER

(watching the shape fly past the glass) …was that the new guy?

BUNNY SOLDIER 2

Was the new guy. Told him not to sit near the window.

A guard eyes one of us a beat too long as we shuffle by.

BUNNY GUARD

Isn't that one walking kind of half-dead?

BUNNY GUARD 2

We all walk half-dead. It's Monday.

THE UNCLE & NINA — WITNESSED

In one of the middle cars, we come upon the Uncle. And he isn't alone. Nina is with him. Standing close. Quiet. She doesn't look like a prisoner. She looks like someone who's lived in this man's shadow a long time. Her face is unreadable.

The Uncle is inspecting the strange mechanism bolted to the engine ahead, humming and pulsing with energy. He turns to Nina with a sudden tenderness that doesn't quite fit his face.

UNCLE

Look at it, Nina. Look at what we're going to do.
When this is finished, the world will be ours again. Mine. Ours.
I'm going to bring back the sun. I am going to save the world.
And you'll be standing right next to me when it happens.

Nina says nothing. Her eyes flick — just for a second — to where we stand in our disguises. She sees us. We see her see us. Then she looks away.

UNCLE

You forgive me, don't you? For earlier. You know I only get angry because I love you.

The most exposed we'll ever see him. A flash of something almost human. Then it's gone. He turns back to the device, all business.

UNCLE

Too much glare. It looks wrong. Fix it.

He pauses. Sniffs the air. His expression flips.

UNCLE

What is that smell? You stink! All of you stink!

He rounds on the nearest soldier — right beside us — and grabs him by the collar.

UNCLE

You. Yes, you. You smell like defeat.

He kicks the soldier off the moving train. A beat. He straightens his coat.

UNCLE

…Now it smells like forest. Better.
Everyone washes. I want you presentable for the end of the world. It's an important event, and it would look bad in the photos.

The car goes silent. Terrified of being made, we press on toward the front, close to Gloomy, avoiding every pair of eyes.

v009: The Uncle's train beats — the "I am going to save the world" tenderness, the smell gag, the soldier kicked off — now happen as we witness them in passing while crossing the wagons, exactly as requested. Curated lines woven: "You smell like defeat… Now it smells like forest," and "Everyone washes… it would look bad in the photos."

STEALING THE LOCOMOTIVE

As we push forward, Nina slips away from the Uncle's side. We don't see her go — one moment she's there, the next the space beside him is empty. She knows this train. She knows exactly how to move through it unseen. She's done this before.

The interior path ahead is blocked. The only way on is outside — the narrow exterior edge of the train as it cuts through the forest. Wind screams. The ground is far below. One wrong step and you fall. We edge along, car by car, toward the engine.

We reach the front. The locomotive. And someone is already there — a figure with a length of pipe, braced, ready. She swings first and asks questions never. We scatter. Then she sees the eyes. The faces under the hoods.

NINA

Wait— (the pipe lowers) …you actually came.

She looks from face to face, something loosening in her.

NINA

You came back. All of you.

Gloomy steps forward. Their eyes meet. The boar plants itself between them and gives Nina a low, distrustful grunt. She barely notices.

NINA

I want to come with you. I can help you find the sun — I know this whole line, every stop, every switch.
Let me prove it. Right now.

She moves to the coupling between the locomotive and the rest of the train and works the mechanism fast, expertly — buying our trust with her own hands. The pin releases. The locomotive tears free and surges ahead up the mountain grade while the rest of the train falls back behind us, shrinking into the dark.

We've stolen the engine. And we've picked up Nina.

v009: Per the new beats — Nina slips ahead to the locomotive unseen (she's the Uncle's spy and knows the train); at the front she attacks with a length of pipe, then softens; she helps us detach the engine to earn our trust and asks to come find the sun. The old "Nina rescued from the last wagon" scene is gone — the detach/coupling beat from 007 lives here now. The boar's distrust starts here and pays off at Pacha's.

SCENE 05

The Bridge — Magic and Falling

Broken Crossing ~5 MIN

The stolen locomotive climbs until the track simply ends — torn away where the mountainside gave out. We leave the engine and go on foot. The path ahead is broken. Part bridge, part ruin, suspended over the forest floor far below.

Gloomy jumps first and lands on the other side. He turns back and reaches out to Nina. When their hands touch, something changes.

v009 — THE MAGIC / ROMANCE

For the first time, we see what happens when they connect. Light spreads across the broken structure. The bridge rebuilds itself through natural energy. Leaves lift into the air. Flowers open and release their petals, drifting in slow, coordinated patterns around the two of them.

And Nina looks at Gloomy differently — like she's seeing him for the first time under all of it. Whatever she came here to do, this wasn't in the plan. There is real magic here, and it catches her off guard. For a moment she forgets to be careful.

Then — the unexpected. The boar, trotting alongside, lifts off the ground too, legs paddling in the air, utterly confused. Gloomy notices him drifting away and gently pulls him back down with one hand while still holding Nina with the other.

The boar lands, snorts, and immediately wedges himself between Nina and Gloomy. He looks up at Nina. He doesn't trust her. He stomps once — a small warning. Gloomy rests a hand on his head; the boar grumbles but stays.

v009: The 007 float/magic beat, now explicitly the moment Nina falls for Gloomy on the bridge ("there's magic there"). It complicates her mission and her heart at once. The boar's distrust of Nina lands here — earned, and paid off at Pacha's.

Gradually, the light fades. Gloomy and Nina drift back down, landing on ground now covered in dry leaves. Around us, movement stirs in the shadows. Huge eyes blink open. The same terrifying barking and deep growls from the dungeon echo nearby.

The Uncle's pets. Indistinct in the dark, watching us, hungry. Nina steps closer behind Gloomy.

NINA

Those things are wild. They're starving.
If we stay here, we're dead. There's no way out.

As the leopards close in and escape seems impossible, a portal opens nearby. Through it, another part of the world. No time to hesitate. We rush through together.

Interactivity: We can touch the flowers as they pass, causing them to release their petals.

SCENE 06

Pacha — What Still Lives

Pacha's Sanctuary ~6 MIN

We arrive inside Pacha's cave, full of life and movement — grown rather than built, like the inside of the Three Generation Tree. Roots twist along the walls and ceiling. Shelves of vines hold strange objects gathered over time. By now Nina moves with us like she belongs — one of the group.

From between the roots, Pacha appears. Slow, bent, fragile, like a very old woman. Her energy shifts constantly — sharp and intense one moment, sleepy and snoring mid-sentence the next. The boar trots straight to her and rubs against her leg. She smiles for the first time and scratches his ears. They know each other well.

PACHA

Oh. Look at you.
I was wondering when you'd show up. Come closer, come closer.
You look tired… so am I, but I've got an excuse. I'm about a thousand years old.

PACHA

The sun didn't leave. Everyone thinks so, but no. Its energy never left.
It went down. Beneath the forest. Into the roots. Into the trees…

She pauses, eyes closing. Nina gently pokes her with a stick to wake her. Pacha blinks, refocuses.

PACHA

Right. Yes! Alone, it cannot wake. Humans and zombies. Together! That's the only way.

She shows us how to connect with the forest, how to call the souls of the trees and feel nature respond when we act as a group rather than as individuals.

Pacha picks up two small stones from a shelf woven into the roots. She holds them up.

PACHA

The portals you've been using — they are not magic. They're not gifts.
They're cracks. Wounds in the world that opened when the sun left.

She strikes the stones gently together. A small spark hangs in the air.

PACHA

Pass through one, and the world remembers you a little less. That's the price. Every door costs something.

Nina looks down. She has used many of them. As the lesson continues, fireflies gather and drift toward the exit, guiding us out.

THE TRAIL / PACHA'S SACRIFICE

Suddenly, we hear them coming. Not the Uncle. The pets. The same terrifying barking from the bridge — closer now. Much closer. And behind it: bunny soldiers. Heavy boots. Weapons.

Pacha turns to Nina. Her face is sad. Not surprised. Sad.

PACHA

(softly) They followed the trail. The little marks. The footprints in the moss. The broken branches.
I know who left them, child.

Nina freezes. Her face goes white.

NINA

(barely) I didn't know they'd come this fast. I swear I didn't… I swear…

PACHA

It's all right. It was supposed to happen this way. Everything happens for a reason.

Nina starts to cry. Real tears. Years of them. The boar growls quietly — confirmation. He always knew.

The pets and bunnies smash into the cave. Gloomy moves to defend Pacha. She gently puts a hand on his arm.

PACHA

Not you. Me.

Pacha steps forward to meet the attack. She raises her hands and the roots respond — wrapping the bunnies, slowing the pets. But there are too many. A leopard reaches her first. She doesn't fight. She lets it. As she falls, she pushes one last wave of energy outward — into Gloomy. His eyes flash. He stumbles.

PACHA

(to Gloomy, weak) The seed. Carry it for me. Wake the sun.

Pacha collapses among the roots. The cave dims. Gloomy lets out a sound none of us have heard from him — raw grief.

Nina opens a portal — and stops. She remembers the cost. She does it anyway, pulling us through at the last possible second as the cave falls into darkness.

Carried from 007, with the new arc paying off: Nina now truly belongs to the group and has fallen for Gloomy, so Pacha's quiet reveal — that Nina left the trail — cuts deeper. Pacha forgives her with compassion, not accusation, and sacrifices herself. Curated line added: Nina's "I didn't know they'd come this fast… I swear."

Interactivity: We raise and lower our hands, as Pacha does, letting particles of light connect us with the natural world.

SCENE 07

The Mountains — The Ascent

Miniature Paths → Rising Ground ~5 MIN

We emerge into a world of fog. The air is cold and heavy; sound feels distant. Nina collapses — not figuratively. Her knees give out. She sits on the wet ground and doesn't get up. Gloomy is barely conscious from Pacha's energy transfer, lying in the moss. The boar stands between them, alert.

No one is leading. For a long time, nothing happens. The fog moves. The mountain breathes far away. We are alone with what we just lost.

Then — small lights. The fireflies. Just a few. They drift through the fog, away from us, deeper into the mountains. Toward something. Nina doesn't look up. The boar looks at us. The Last Standing. He waits.

One of us has to take the first step. (The audience's first true act of leadership. No music. No narration. Only the choice to walk.)

GRAVEKEEPER (V.O.)

No one's carrying you from here on. The ones who walked first are gone. Now the first step is yours. It always was.

When in doubt… follow the fireflies.

v009 — NEW ENDING SEQUENCE

The miniature fades. We're back at full scale, high on the mountain, following glowing footprints through thinning mist. Then Nina stops. She lifts her arm and points.

NINA

(quietly) There. It's here. The sun — it's inside the mountain.

A single clean beam of light cuts through the fog from a gap in the rock ahead. We all see it. And the moment we do, the ground begins to shake.

Cracks race across the stone. The terrain breaks apart into blocks — great slabs of mountain shearing loose from one another, tilting, dropping. Nina shouts over the noise.

NINA

Solid ground! Get to solid ground — move!

We scramble across the breaking slabs to one wide block that still holds. It separates from all the rest. And then — it begins to rise. Our slab of earth lifts into the air, carrying us upward, while everything around us collapses and falls away into the fog below.

We climb higher and higher, the valley shrinking beneath us, the beams of light guiding us up. Finally the block slows and stops, high against the mountain face, level with the mouth of an enormous cavern.

The beams of light seem to fade. The mist settles. We step off the block and into the cavern.

And that's when the Uncle appears.

He forces his way in behind us, unhurried, certain. He turns directly to Nina.

UNCLE

You chose him over me. And every step you took in his direction brought me exactly where I wanted to be.

UNCLE

(to all of us) Thanks for finding the sun, by the way. I spent years looking. You did it in an afternoon. Desperation makes an excellent guide.

SCENE 08

Final Confrontation — Awakening the Sun

The Sun Cavern ~4 MIN

The Uncle moves to activate his machine. The device hums louder, pulling energy from the void below. Suddenly, a portal opens above at 45 degrees. Gloomy returns through it in a wide, uncontrolled leap, landing hard between the Uncle and Nina, barely upright. His body is damaged. His eyes burn — but with Pacha's light now, not just his own.

UNCLE

You again! That cannot be. You were dead.
(steadies himself) …though, honestly, that was never much of a guarantee with you lot.

The Uncle tries to defend himself, but Gloomy drives him back, and back, toward the edge. Nina moves forward, trying to stop it.

NINA

Stop! Please. Stop.

UNCLE

Stay out of it.

v009 — THE FALL

OUTRO

A New Dawn

Shadow Miniature Epilogue ~2 MIN

The Gravekeeper's voice returns, calm and distant, echoing gently through the space. The pheasant is with him again, perched on a headstone.

The sun has returned. Nature is breathing again.

GRAVEKEEPER

You walked where nothing was meant to survive. You stood together when everything was falling apart.
In this world, that's nearly a miracle. Nearly.

Not all of us came back. Some gave the last of what they had so this morning could exist.

This world is fragile. It always was. Half of it woke up. The other half still sleeps, out there, beyond the mountains.

The show's over. Go home.
